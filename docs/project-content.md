### 01 - Problem

Master Salons is a small business located in Woodbridge, Virginia and became the first local multi-salon business in 2010. Owners Ric and Alma Wyatt approached our program with a set of improvements in mind, including:

**Reception Services**

Customers often times experience confusion upon arrival due to the non-traditional salon model. Without a first point-of-contact, customers flock to the nearest salon professional for questions. This takes time away from a professional’s booked appointments or administrative responsibilities.

**Modernized Website**

The current website is static and outdated. Users reported difficulty navigating to different salon professionals and learning more about the services offered.

**Check-in system**

Customers reported waiting for extended periods of time before their appointed salon professional is aware of their arrival.

**Informative system inclusive to new and existing customers**

Walk-in customers often have questions about what a multi-salon suite is, how it works, what is offered, and pricing.

**Sponsor criteria**

- Easily maintainable for a non-technical person
- Security for hardware and software
- Cost effective

Slide 11 labels the historical website image “WEBSITE PRIOR TO CAPSTONE.”

### 02 - Goal

Create a system that supports existing business processes by introducing technological solutions.

### 03 - Process

Over the course of the first semester, my team met with our sponsor weekly to learn more about their business processes, pinpointing key pain points and opportunities for improvement. Upon our findings, we researched competition, design, potential solutions, technologies, and quantified the time and cost savings to the current process while maintaining frequent communication with the sponsor. After analyzing our potential improvements, we decided to package our ideas into 3 solutions:

**1 - Digital Reception Kiosk System**

To improve the customer experience on arrival, we sought to provide a digital experience which includes features such as check-in, notifications on arrival, suite directions, informative service and pricing options, portfolio display, appointment booking.

**2 - Modernized Website**

We created mockups of the website based on the sponsor preferences and user feedback. Modernizing it to be informative to customers looking to book or salon professionals looking to lease a space.

**3 - Kiosk Hardware**

With the digital receptionist kiosk, comes the hardware attached. To minimize workload on our sponsor, our team will be configuring the hardware, ensuring security features, and compatibility with the software.

Retain the proposed tense for the reception kiosk and hardware. The completed website artifact identified in the process is the set of mockups.

### 04 - Challenge.. Starting From Square One!

Master Salons wasn’t our first sponsor... For the first half of the semester, we had done research, created prototypes, and planned for implementation with George Mason University’s Disability Services. Due to unforseen circumstances, we had to choose a new sponsor leading us to Master Salons. With about a month left of the semester, the team took a hit in motivation and scrambled to get 3 months worth of work done in just one month.

With major support from our professor, TA, and the team, we worked day and night to ensure we caught up with the same if not better quality of work for our sponsor. In the end, we finished the semester strong. This experience reminded me that discipline triumphs motivation, especially when you have a team who will push to the finish line together.

### 05 - My Role as Team Lead

As team lead, my goal was to push the team from idea to development, which meant setting up foundations for success. This meant establishing team communication expectations, setting up agile workflows, giving constructive criticism, and most importantly leading by example.

My biggest takeaway was learning the balance between being a friend and being a lead... What do I mean by that? Often times when team members become friends, we struggle to provide critique or call out behaviors that may hurt the team as a whole in fear of retaliation. I found that there’s a sweet spot somewhere in between by prioritizing the improvements that are necessary to the teams success as a whole, not by micromanaging based on personal preference. The team lead is not a tyrant, but a guide to move things along in an efficient and sustainable way.

### 03 - Process

I had begun interviewing multiple parties including the multimedia team to understand the current workflows, frustrations, and the vision. Alongside, these interviews, I had initially researched existing IT and software solutions to reference, and spoken to numerous IT professionals around the company including senior software developers, power platform developers, the IT department, and product owners. I wanted to get a better understanding of not only the technical side of what’s going on behind the scenes, but also how IT professionals approach business problems.

I gathered information on the limitations, existing technology, security, and budget restrictions. What I had initially thought was going to be a simple asset management application had developed into a greater solution. The root of the problem had to be fixed while considering the limitations, leading me to my three big solutions:

**1 - Develop and implement a taxonomy system (with documentation for the media team to maintain)**

The reference describes centralizing assets and retaining archives.

**2 - Utilize the media computer as a stand in server for assets and host the application**

The department did not have a server to host applications nor to store large amounts of data. The best solution was to utilize the beefy computer.

**3 - Digital Asset Management Application**

This portion of the solution solves the initial pain point expressed by the media team.

This project will focus on the application portion of my solution.

I researched existing digital asset management systems such as Aprimo, Adobe, and referenced GitHub repos, APIs, and existing technologies I could just plug in rather than building completely form scratch. With the help of ChatGPT and senior developers, I was able to successfully create a digital asset management system that read files directly from the CPU rather than uploading it directly. I made this choice due to storage limitations and security compliance.

Internal access and storage configuration is excluded.

**Post deployment**

I currently maintain the application and I’m working on implementing AI autotagging features using Gemma 4 e4b and Ollama to automate the manual video tagging process. I researched AI models, implemented base layer security, and tested its VLM capabilities on sample frames. My findings led me to improve prompting and research model training for specific objects. This project is unfinished and this page will continue to be updated.
